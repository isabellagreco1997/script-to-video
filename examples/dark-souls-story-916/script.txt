Dark Souls came out in 2011, and almost nobody at the publisher believed in it.

It was the spiritual sequel to Demon's Souls, a PlayStation game that Sony had refused to release outside Japan, because it looked too hard to sell.

The director, Hidetaka Miyazaki, wanted one thing. A world where dying teaches you something. You keep your progress. You lose your souls. And you get exactly one chance to walk back and reclaim them.

There is no difficulty setting. There is no pause button. And the map folds back on itself, so every shortcut you unlock feels like you discovered it.

It sold more than two million copies in its first year. It spawned two sequels, Bloodborne, Sekiro, and Elden Ring. And it gave its name to an entire genre.

The message you see most is: you died. The message it is actually sending is: try again. You know more now.
