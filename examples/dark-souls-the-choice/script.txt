You reach the end of Dark Souls, defeat Gwyn, and discover the final boss was never the real question.
It is whether his world deserves to continue.

Before kingdoms, there were ancient dragons and a world without change.
Then the First Flame appeared, bringing life and death, light and dark.
Gwyn, Nito, and the Witch of Izalith claimed powerful souls.
With Seath's betrayal, they defeated the dragons and began the Age of Fire.
But another being, the Furtive Pygmy, found the Dark Soul: humanity's inheritance.

Eventually, the flame weakened.
Gwyn feared an age where humanity would replace the gods.
He burned himself to keep the fire alive.
It bought time. It did not solve the problem.

By your arrival, Lordran is crumbling.
The undead return after death, but many lose their minds and become Hollows.
You escape the Undead Asylum and reach Firelink Shrine.
A prophecy sends you after two bells: one above, one deep below.
Ring both, and the serpent Frampt directs you towards Anor Londo.
Beyond Ornstein and Smough waits the Lordvessel.

To open the way to Gwyn, you must gather great souls.
Nito. Seath. The Four Kings.
And the Bed of Chaos, born from the Witch's failed attempt to recreate the flame.
These are the broken remains of the old order.

Frampt calls your sacrifice salvation.
But another serpent, Kaathe, offers a different story: let the fire fade, and humanity can inherit the world.
Neither guide is neutral.

At the Kiln, Gwyn fights with a burning sword, almost everything else spent.
Defeat him, and link the fire by burning yourself.
Or walk away, becoming the Dark Lord.
Dark Souls gives you power to choose.
It never gives you certainty.
